# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of automotive industry news for readers who track OEM strategy, vehicle technology, the EV transition, and the automotive supply chain. The reader wants industry direction, corporate strategy, new technology, and financial results, not individual vehicle announcements.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Groundbreaking.** Moves that reshape the industry: major OEM mergers, alliances, or restructurings, landmark EV or emissions policy, breakthrough battery or autonomous-driving technology reaching production, or a shift that changes the competitive structure.
- **7-8: High value.** Material earnings and guidance changes, large investments or plant decisions, significant OEM strategy shifts, consequential supplier or semiconductor developments, major partnerships, and concrete sales, production, or market-share data with meaningful baselines.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible incremental developments that help readers understand an OEM, technology, or market trend but have limited breadth or urgency, such as moderate technology updates, regional policy adjustments, or narrower market data.
- **3-4: Low priority.** New model launches and debuts, concept-car reveals, auto-show and exhibition coverage, facelifts and trim updates, test drives and vehicle reviews, pricing or dealer promotions, and motorsports results.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spy shots and rumor reporting, purely promotional or marketing content, unreviewed speculation, and material with no industry or strategic substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Prioritize items with strategic and competitive relevance to global OEMs and the automotive supply chain. Consider whether the item changes industry direction, corporate strategy, technology capability, or financial outlook, and whether it provides concrete figures with meaningful baselines. New-vehicle announcements, motor-show coverage, and motorsport items belong in the 3-4 band or lower even when prominent, because they do not convey industry strategy or technology direction. Distinguish reported facts from forecasts and claims. Do not reward exaggerated headlines or marketing language.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
